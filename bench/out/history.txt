Most accounts agree that the Industrial Revolution was a major turning point in human history. It began in Britain in the late 18th century and brought mechanized manufacturing, which greatly increased productivity. As workers moved to cities to find jobs, urbanization sped up. The period also caused major social problems. One of these problems was poor working conditions. It reshaped economies and societies alike, and its effects still influence the modern world. In most cases, factory owners probably cared more about output than safety, and workers tended to face long hours in dangerous environments.
